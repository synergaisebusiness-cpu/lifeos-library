Macro backdrop

The dominant macro driver this week was geopolitical, not domestic data. The US-Iran conflict (active since February) escalated sharply in late July — a Houthi Red Sea attack sent oil above $100/bbl on July 23 — before de-escalating just as sharply: the State Department issued a fresh Middle East travel warning Aug 1, but by the evening of Aug 2 President Trump announced negotiations with Iran would begin today (Aug 3), calling a Strait of Hormuz deal "imminent." Oil slid nearly 5% and US equity futures rallied on the news — S&P futures +0.4%, Nasdaq 100 futures +0.65%, Dow futures +0.39% as of Monday morning. No permanent resolution has been reached. (CNN, Aug 1, Benzinga, Aug 2, Bloomberg, Aug 2, Yahoo Finance, Aug 3)

FOMC: the Fed held rates unchanged at its July 28-29 meeting, with Chair Kevin Warsh's majority favoring a hold until at least September, when officials will have both July and August CPI in hand. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan filed a formal dissent July 31. Jackson Hole is confirmed for Aug 27-29; Warsh said his keynote is "a blank piece of paper right now." (CNBC, Jul 29, Dallas Fed)

Q2 GDP: the advance estimate, released July 30, showed real GDP growing just 1.5% annualized, below the 2.1% consensus and down from Q1's 2.1%. International trade subtracted a full point from growth as imports surged 11.5%, partly offset by 4.5% export growth. Second estimate due Aug 26. (BEA, Advisor Perspectives)

June PCE: also released July 30 — headline PCE -0.1% m/m / +3.7% y/y (down from May's 4.1%); core PCE +0.1% m/m / +3.3% y/y (down from May's 3.4%), both roughly in line with consensus, though one analyst flagged that June PCE "did not show the same cooling reflected in the CPI report earlier this month," warning of renewed energy-driven upward pressure. (Fox Business)

Equities: markets rebounded hard from the prior week's FOMC-driven selloff — S&P 500 +1.7% to 7,437.64 and Nasdaq +2.8% to 25,122.18 on July 30 (Microsoft/Amazon-led), Dow +277pts to 52,485 on July 31, closing out a fourth straight winning month for the Dow. Of ~300 S&P 500 companies reported by early August, 85% beat estimates with aggregate profit growth tracking above 47%. Monday Aug 3 closing levels were not yet available at time of writing. (CNBC, Jul 30, CNBC weekly outlook, Jul 31)

Jobs report: the July 2026 Employment Situation report is not yet out — scheduled for Friday, Aug 7, outside this week's window. For reference, June's report showed only +57K jobs, well below the downwardly-revised May figure of 129K; one preview pegs July consensus at a modest +90K (a forecast, not a fact). (BLS calendar, Trading Economics)

Sector breakdowns

AI compute & semiconductors (NVDA, AMD, MU, AVGO, ASML)

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had its worst month since October 2008, plunging 21% in July before staging an 8.3% two-day rally into month-end — the index remains ~23% off its June 22 record, with every constituent still red over the stretch. The volatility sits oddly alongside strong underlying fundamentals: global semiconductor sales hit a record $120.6bn in May (+104% YoY, a 15th consecutive monthly record). (Bloomberg, 24/7 Wall St)

Nvidia hit a fresh China flashpoint July 31: Bloomberg reported that Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 (a 2.8T-parameter open-weight model) was trained using a ~20,000-Nvidia-chip cluster provided via an Alibaba cloud arrangement, and White House tech-policy chief Michael Kratsios alleged Blackwell processors were acquired illegally, possibly routed through Thailand. Multiple researchers publicly disputed the technical plausibility of the underlying distillation claim. NVDA closed July 31 down 0.9%, in line with the broader chip whipsaw. (Bloomberg, Tom's Hardware)

AMD had no fresh company-specific news this window beyond pre-earnings positioning ahead of tomorrow's (Aug 4) print — Wedbush lifted its PT to a Street-leading $600 (from $450), raising FY26 estimates to $49.2bn revenue / $7.22 EPS. (Yahoo/Wedbush)

Micron had a genuinely volatile week: shares rallied on bullish read-throughs from Samsung, Microsoft and Lam Research (Samsung warned memory supply could stay tight into 2028), then reversed to close July 31 down 5.9% on rising concern that rival HBM producers are ramping yields fast enough to pressure Micron's AI-memory pricing power. (Benzinga, StocksToTrade)

ASML rebounded +6.8% on July 30 as analysts converged on a "modest threat" read of the China DUV story: even if China hits its 2027 target of ~20 domestic immersion-DUV tools, the estimated revenue impact to ASML is only ~€1.4bn (~2.4% of group sales), against China's 29% share of ASML's 2025 sales. BofA called the initial selloff an "overreaction" with 70%+ upside still intact. (Yahoo/GuruFocus, Motley Fool)

Broadcom had no material news this window; its fiscal Q3 quarter closed Aug 2, with results due early September.

AI networking & power (ANET, VRT, GEV, CEG)

Arista Networks rallied sharply into its Aug 4 print — +7-8% on July 30 and a further +4.5% on July 31 — on broad AI-networking optimism (800G adoption commentary, hyperscaler capex reaffirmation) rather than any single company catalyst. Coverage also flagged an estimated ~$450m of trailing-90-day insider share sales (including the CEO) as a sentiment headwind that had contributed to a -6.9% single-day drop July 29, just before the rebound. Software / data / AI (CRWD, DDOG, PLTR, ORCL, MSFT, GOOGL, META, AMZN)

The mega-cap earnings reaction this week diverged more sharply than the last briefing's snapshot suggested. Microsoft's stock surged 15.5% on July 30 — the largest single-day market-cap gain in stock-market history (~$450-480bn added) — after Azure revenue crossed $100bn for the first time at 43% growth (guided to 45% next quarter), Copilot seats passed 30 million, and FY26 capex guidance came in effectively flat in dollar terms after an accounting change spread data-center/office costs over a longer period (headline shifted from ~$190bn to ~$175bn), even as Q4 capex itself rose 70% YoY to $41bn. This stands in stark contrast to Meta's free-cash-flow collapse the same week (already flagged last briefing) — commentary is now framing this as an explicit "AI trade split" between hyperscalers, with Amazon's post-earnings surge (AWS +36.7% YoY, best stock pop since 2012) landing on the Microsoft side of that split. Aggregate 2026 capex across the top hyperscalers is now tracking toward ~$700-750bn, with rising scrutiny of debt-financed buildout — one Fast Money commentator described some of the financing as "issuing near junk." (Motley Fool, CFO Dive, CNBC, 24/7 Wall St)

Oracle announced an expanded Google Cloud partnership July 30, bringing Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite and Gemini 3.5 Flash into Oracle Fusion Applications' AI Agent Studio, Fusion Cloud Applications and NetSuite — stock jumped as much as 8.2% on the news. (Oracle, Bloomberg)

Alphabet had two notable items: it rolled back a newly-launched Google Earth AI image-generation feature July 31, a day after launch, after users generated altered satellite imagery including a fabricated nuclear plant in Iran — the feature is paused while stronger guardrails are built. Separately, reports (Jul 30) indicate Google is backstopping roughly $15bn of financing for Anthropic's Nexus Data Centers project in Hubbard, Texas — an ~$14bn bridge loan plus revolver led by Morgan Stanley, with Google guaranteeing lease/power-purchase obligations across four Anthropic data-center leases (tied to a 1.6GW on-site gas plant) in exchange for an estimated ~20% project equity stake. (Bloomberg, CNBC)

Exponential thesis watch

Compute: Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness LP — an ex-OpenAI-run fund built explicitly on the AGI-compute-buildout thesis at ~4x leverage, peak NAV ~$45bn, +439% return through June — got margin-called (Goldman, JPMorgan, BofA) during the SOX's 28.6% drawdown from its June 22 peak, and sold its entire public-equities book (estimates range $16-35bn) to Citadel in a single block trade July 30, losing roughly three-quarters of assets in days. Bitcoin-mining-turned-AI-hosting names (TeraWulf, IREN, Applied Digital) fell 33-38% in sympathy while spot BTC held relatively stable — a notable compute/crypto-infrastructure correlation data point worth researching further. As Azhar's coverage notes, this is a case study in the fragility of leveraged bets on the thesis, distinct from whether the underlying AGI-capex thesis itself is right — worth keeping those two questions separate when reading the story. The Nvidia/Moonshot-Alibaba chip-smuggling allegation (above) is a second, live China-compute flashpoint alongside the ongoing DUV-lithography story. (CryptoTicker, Exponential View)

Energy: Commonwealth Fusion Systems raised $1bn on July 30, taking its total raised toward ~$4bn for commercial fusion machines — a concrete new capital data point for the fusion leg of the thesis, worth researching as a candidate for the picks-and-shovels angle. The DOE selected 278 projects for its new "Genesis Mission" AI-driven scientific discovery initiative (spanning fusion, nuclear, critical minerals and chip design) — described as the largest response to a DOE funding opportunity in its history, with Princeton Plasma Physics Lab leading AI-operator work on electron-cyclotron heating. The Fusion Industry Association separately reported a record $4.48bn in trailing-year fundraising across the sector, with 71% of surveyed companies expecting grid-connected fusion plants in the 2030s. (thefusionreport.substack.com, Jul 31)

Manufacturing / robotics: Tesla's escalated 10-million-units/year Optimus messaging and BYD's confirmed August humanoid-robot debut both point to accelerating ambition and competition in humanoid robotics even as actual volume production remains pre-scale for every player. Worth researching how credible BYD's entry is relative to the more established field (Boston Dynamics/Hyundai, Figure, Unitree, AgiBot) — a first major Chinese automaker committing to a public date is a different signal than the existing robotics-pure-play announcements.

Biology: no genuinely new development surfaced specifically for the biology convergence pillar this window — flagging that explicitly rather than padding. The most recent dated activity remains the Scribe Therapeutics IPO and ex-OpenAI drug-discovery talk noted previously, with the broader biotech IPO window (see medtech section) continuing to open as a secondary signal.
