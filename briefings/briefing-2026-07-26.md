## Top of mind

- **This was peak earnings week for the AI-capex complex, and the market didn't like the bill.** Alphabet (-7%) and Tesla (-14.5%) both fell hard on July 22-23 despite strong headline growth, as investors balked at rising capex (Alphabet raised 2026 capex to $195-205B) and margin compression (Tesla's operating margin fell to 1.4%). The "Magnificent Seven" shed ~$800bn in a single session (July 23).
- **Next week is the real test**: Microsoft, Meta and Vertiv report July 29; Apple, Amazon and Schneider Electric report July 30 — all landing in the same week as the FOMC decision (July 28-29) and the Q2 GDP/PCE releases (July 30). This is the most data-dense week of the cycle.
- **The AI-infrastructure "picks and shovels" layer (networking, power, memory) is holding up better than the GPU layer** — Arista, GE Vernova and Micron all have durable, contracted demand (Arista's $3.5bn AI revenue target, Micron's $22bn non-cancelable HBM contracts, GE Vernova's $176bn backlog) even as sentiment around NVDA/AMD/TSLA wobbles on valuation and margin questions.
- **AMD had its biggest two days of the year** at its July 22-23 Advancing AI event — real hyperscaler commitments (Microsoft, Meta, OpenAI, Oracle) for its Helios rack platform, not roadmap slides, positioning it as a credible #2 to Nvidia.
- **Builder sentiment is diverging**: Jensen Huang says the chip industry needs to grow 5-10x and sees no bust "for a while"; Mark Zuckerberg privately told staff AI agent progress "hasn't really accelerated" as hoped. Same week, same thesis, different temperature readings from two of its biggest backers.

## Sector breakdowns

### AI compute & semiconductors (NVDA, AMD, MU, AVGO, ASML)

**Nvidia** underperformed peers this cycle — down ~17-18% from its May record — as China exposure remains a real headwind (Jensen Huang has said Nvidia has "largely conceded" China's advanced-chip market to Huawei, though the US resumed issuing H20 export licenses this month). Offsetting that: a denied rumor of a Kyber NVL144 delay, Goldman flagging Nvidia's 21.7x forward P/E as cheap relative to its own 5-year average of 72x, and — the big one — a $500bn-plus LOI with SK Group (SK Telecom AI factories, SK hynix HBM4 supply) alongside a Japan "Physical AI Coalition" signing Toyota, Fujitsu, Kawasaki, FANUC and others. Nvidia reiterated ~$1 trillion in confirmed AI chip demand through 2027 from Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Meta. ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/08/nvidia-stock-is-down-17-from-its-artif/), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/nvidia-and-sk-group-enter-usd500-billion-ai-partnership-plan-to-supercharge-ai-infrastructure-with-next-gen-memory-and-massive-ai-factories))

**AMD** had its biggest two days of the year at its July 22-23 Advancing AI event: EPYC "Venice" (Zen 6, TSMC 2nm) launched commercially, and the Instinct MI450 series/Helios rack platform ($5-5.5M per rack) was confirmed for H2 2026 shipment to Microsoft, Meta, Oracle and OpenAI. Microsoft separately confirmed it will run Azure AI workloads on Helios; Rosenblatt raised its target to $655 and UBS to $700. This is AMD's most credible positioning yet as a #2 to Nvidia in AI accelerators, on the back of a 6GW OpenAI deal potentially worth up to $100bn cumulatively. ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321257/20260722/amd-advancing-ai-2026-opens-zen-6-venice-helios-open-ai-rack-bet.htm), [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/amd-to-supply-openai-with-6gw-worth-of-gpus-plans-1gw-deployment-starting-in-2026/))

**Micron** looks like the cleanest read on the HBM supply squeeze underpinning the whole AI-chip complex: HBM capacity is effectively sold out through 2026-2027, with 16 strategic customer arrangements worth ~$22bn in non-cancelable memory supply, plus a multi-part Anthropic partnership (co-optimization, supply agreement, and a Series H investment). The stock still fell ~20% from its post-earnings peak amid the broader chip sell-off before Morgan Stanley and BofA both reiterated "buy the dip." ([FX Leaders](https://www.fxleaders.com/news/2026/07/21/micron-mu-stock-news-ai-memory-demand-july-21-2026/), [TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262047242-micron-mu-stock-recovery-july-22-2026-morgan-stanley-bofa-buy-22b-contracts-tradingkey))

**Broadcom** disclosed a new $30bn-plus, multi-year deal with Apple to produce 15 billion-plus custom ASICs through 2031, including collaboration on Apple's first dedicated AI server chip (as early as 2027) — Apple is ~20% of Broadcom's revenue, so this materially de-risks the relationship. Despite record Q2 numbers (AI-chip revenue +143% YoY), the stock still dropped ~4% amid sector-wide selling. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/08/apple-commits-30-billion-to-broadcom-for-us-chipmaking-push.html))

**ASML** raised FY2026 guidance to €43-45bn and committed to 30% annual EUV/DUV capacity growth for the next two years — one of the more reliable leading indicators that leading-edge chip demand isn't slowing at the equipment layer even as downstream AI-stock sentiment wobbles.

Analyst sentiment is now mixed at the margin — KeyBanc and Baird initiated bullish coverage in July, but RBC cut its target to $418 from $435 — suggesting some valuation caution ahead of the July 29 print even as the underlying growth story stays intact. **Cross-cutting theme**: multiple July reports frame grid capacity — not capital or chip supply — as the binding constraint on AI data-center growth. New grid connections in Northern Virginia can face waits of up to 14 years; roughly half of data centers scheduled to start in 2026 are estimated to face cancellation or delay due to power constraints. Gartner projects global data-center electricity demand will exceed 1,000 TWh in 2026, double the 2023 baseline. This is the structural tailwind behind GE Vernova, Constellation and the electrical-equipment names below. ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/07/ai-data-centers-demand-expansion/))

## Exponential thesis watch

**Compute**: The Nvidia-SK Group $500bn LOI and Alphabet's reported "Frozen v2" custom TPU effort (targeting 6-10x tokens/watt by 2028) both point the same direction — every major compute buyer is now hedging against single-vendor dependence, whether through vertical integration (Google, Meta's "Iris" chips, Amazon's own silicon) or mega-scale supply locks (SK hynix HBM4, Micron's $22bn contracts). Azeem Azhar's 95%-undeployed-GPU data point is the counter-signal worth researching further — a gap between shipped and deployed compute would matter a great deal to anyone modeling near-term chip demand.

**Energy**: The grid, not capital or chips, is emerging as the AI buildout's real bottleneck — 14-year interconnection queues in Northern Virginia and roughly half of 2026's planned data centers at risk of delay. This is the through-line connecting Constellation's SMR investment (Blue Energy), GE Vernova's gas-turbine backlog, and the Trump administration's Saudi nuclear deal (however uncertain that agreement now looks) and its $200m SMR-acceleration initiative. Worth researching as candidates: other SMR developers and gas-turbine/grid-equipment suppliers beyond the names already held, given how tight turbine and switchgear capacity has become.

**Manufacturing / robotics**: Japan's ¥80 trillion national robotics push (targeting 10 million AI robots by 2040, with Toyota, FANUC, Kawasaki and others now in Nvidia's Cosmos Coalition) is a bigger, more concrete industrial-policy commitment than anything comparable in the US to date. Combined with Boston Dynamics' simplified new Atlas and Figure's BMW pilot, July 2026 looks like the moment humanoid robotics started producing real (if small) unit volumes rather than demos. Musk's own admission that Optimus's ramp will be "long and flat" given a near-total lack of a mature component supply chain is a useful reality check on how far the *industrial base* for humanoid robotics still has to go — that supply chain (actuators, sensors, specialized components) is itself a research candidate worth tracking as a picks-and-shovels angle, separate from the robot-makers themselves.

**Biology**: Isomorphic Labs' $2.1bn Series B (AI drug discovery, DeepMind spinout) was the largest single biotech financing tracked this cycle, alongside a broader rebound in biotech IPO activity. Merck KGaA's $11.3bn acquisition of Bio-Techne signals continued consolidation upstream of Thermo Fisher's own tools business — worth watching whether further M&A compresses TMO's competitive moat or simply validates the category's growth.
