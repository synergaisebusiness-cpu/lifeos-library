Sector breakdowns

AI compute & semiconductors (NVDA, AMD, MU, AVGO, ASML)

Nvidia underperformed peers this cycle — down ~17-18% from its May record — as China exposure remains a real headwind (Jensen Huang has said Nvidia has "largely conceded" China's advanced-chip market to Huawei, though the US resumed issuing H20 export licenses this month). Offsetting that: a denied rumor of a Kyber NVL144 delay, Goldman flagging Nvidia's 21.7x forward P/E as cheap relative to its own 5-year average of 72x, and — the big one — a $500bn-plus LOI with SK Group (SK Telecom AI factories, SK hynix HBM4 supply) alongside a Japan "Physical AI Coalition" signing Toyota, Fujitsu, Kawasaki, FANUC and others. Nvidia reiterated ~$1 trillion in confirmed AI chip demand through 2027 from Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Meta. (Motley Fool, Tom's Hardware)

AMD had its biggest two days of the year at its July 22-23 Advancing AI event: EPYC "Venice" (Zen 6, TSMC 2nm) launched commercially, and the Instinct MI450 series/Helios rack platform ($5-5.5M per rack) was confirmed for H2 2026 shipment to Microsoft, Meta, Oracle and OpenAI. Microsoft separately confirmed it will run Azure AI workloads on Helios; Rosenblatt raised its target to $655 and UBS to $700. This is AMD's most credible positioning yet as a #2 to Nvidia in AI accelerators, on the back of a 6GW OpenAI deal potentially worth up to $100bn cumulatively. (TechTimes, DCD)

Micron looks like the cleanest read on the HBM supply squeeze underpinning the whole AI-chip complex: HBM capacity is effectively sold out through 2026-2027, with 16 strategic customer arrangements worth ~$22bn in non-cancelable memory supply, plus a multi-part Anthropic partnership (co-optimization, supply agreement, and a Series H investment). The stock still fell ~20% from its post-earnings peak amid the broader chip sell-off before Morgan Stanley and BofA both reiterated "buy the dip." (FX Leaders, TradingKey)

Broadcom disclosed a new $30bn-plus, multi-year deal with Apple to produce 15 billion-plus custom ASICs through 2031, including collaboration on Apple's first dedicated AI server chip (as early as 2027) — Apple is ~20% of Broadcom's revenue, so this materially de-risks the relationship. Despite record Q2 numbers (AI-chip revenue +143% YoY), the stock still dropped ~4% amid sector-wide selling. (CNBC)

ASML raised FY2026 guidance to €43-45bn and committed to 30% annual EUV/DUV capacity growth for the next two years — one of the more reliable leading indicators that leading-edge chip demand isn't slowing at the equipment layer even as downstream AI-stock sentiment wobbles.

AI networking & power (ANET, VRT, GEV, CEG)

Arista Networks is the cleanest pure-play read on AI networking capex. CEO Jayshree Ullal called current demand "the best I've ever seen in my Arista tenure" (while flagging supply constraints across wafers, silicon, optics and memory) and the company raised its 2026 AI revenue target to $3.5bn. KeyBanc, BofA, Morgan Stanley and TD Cowen all raised price targets in early July (cluster now $190-210). Note: CEO Ullal disclosed a routine ~$43.9m share sale on July 10, ahead of the Aug 4 print. (TIKR, Trefis)

Vertiv more than doubled in H1 2026 (+106.7%) on data-center power/cooling demand, building a $15bn backlog on 252% order growth. Analyst sentiment is now mixed at the margin — KeyBanc and Baird initiated bullish coverage in July, but RBC cut its target to $418 from $435 — suggesting some valuation caution ahead of the July 29 print even as the underlying growth story stays intact. (Motley Fool)

GE Vernova raised full-year revenue guidance to $45.5-46.5bn after a record $24.2bn order quarter; data-center-related orders alone have exceeded $5bn in 2026, more than double all of 2025. A wave of analyst price-target hikes (RBC, Morgan Stanley, Guggenheim, JPMorgan, Bernstein, all July 22-25) followed even though EPS missed — the market is betting on gas-turbine and grid-equipment scarcity persisting for years, not on near-term margin.

Constellation Energy cleared 18,875 MW in the July 14 PJM capacity auction for the 2028-2029 delivery year and made its first-ever US SMR developer investment (Blue Energy, a modular reactor-agnostic plant designer aiming for ~36-month build times). CEG rose ~5% on July 22 amid a broader nuclear-sector rally tied to the US-Saudi nuclear cooperation agreement (see Nuclear & Grid below). Q2 earnings land Aug 6, outside this window. (World Nuclear News, NAI 500)

Cross-cutting theme: multiple July reports frame grid capacity — not capital or chip supply — as the binding constraint on AI data-center growth. New grid connections in Northern Virginia can face waits of up to 14 years; roughly half of data centers scheduled to start in 2026 are estimated to face cancellation or delay due to power constraints. Gartner projects global data-center electricity demand will exceed 1,000 TWh in 2026, double the 2023 baseline. This is the structural tailwind behind GE Vernova, Constellation and the electrical-equipment names below. (Help Net Security)

Nuclear & grid (CEG, CCJ)

The dominant nuclear-policy story: the Trump administration signed a civil nuclear cooperation ("123") agreement with Saudi Arabia on July 22, triggering a broad nuclear-stock rally — but Trump then added a last-minute condition tying finalization to Saudi-Israel normalization, throwing the deal into doubt pending Congressional approval and raising concerns about a pathway to Saudi domestic uranium enrichment. (NPR, Washington Post)

Cameco temporarily suspended mining at Cigar Lake on July 1 after a sulfuric-acid plant failure at the mill that processes its ore; production has since resumed and 2026 guidance (17.5-18.0M lb U3O8) is unchanged. Cameco also closed its purchase of TEPCO's 5% Cigar Lake stake, lifting its ownership to 57.4%. The stock is down ~8.6% over the past month on the disruption plus broader nuclear/SMR sentiment softness; uranium spot pricing sources diverge meaningfully this month ($71-86/lb depending on tracker), so treat any single spot figure with caution. (TipRanks, Simply Wall St)

Broader SMR-sector context: Oklo, NuScale and X-Energy fell >8% on July 16 on profit-taking and milestone-slippage concerns, then rallied (OKLO +6%, XE +7%, SMR +9%) after being selected alongside Microsoft and Nvidia for a ~$200m Trump-administration initiative to accelerate reactor deployment for AI data centers. A more skeptical counterpoint from the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists (July 24) argues SMRs cannot realistically fill data-center power needs on the timeline AI demand requires — worth holding both views in mind. (TipRanks, Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists)

Hyperscaler capex is the story of the cycle. Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta collectively are on track for roughly $700bn+ combined 2026 capex — Meta's guide alone nearly doubled from 2025 ($72bn) to $125-145bn. Notably, on July 1 Meta stock jumped 9% on news of "Meta Compute," a new unit selling spare AI compute externally — direct competition with AWS/Azure/GCP — while Meta also signed a $21bn CoreWeave deal and is developing in-house "Iris" chips for September 2026 production. Alphabet, meanwhile, is reportedly developing a next-gen "Frozen v2" TPU architecture (per The Information) targeting 6-10x more tokens per watt by 2028 — a direct shot at Nvidia's dominance. (CNBC, CNBC)

Oracle has seen its market cap fall from an $877bn September 2025 peak to ~$383bn by mid-July, reflecting concern that AI-datacenter capex is outrunning monetization (FY2026 cloud revenue +39% to $34bn, but free cash flow turned negative). CrowdStrike completed a 4-for-1 stock split July 2 and rallied on AI-security price-target hikes (UBS to $235, Stifel to $220), but then fell for seven straight sessions into July 23 — a reminder that even well-liked names aren't immune to the broader AI-valuation jitters. Caterpillar raised its quarterly dividend 8% but flagged a full-year tariff headwind that's escalated to roughly $2.6bn (mostly Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs) — underlying construction/mining/power-equipment demand remains solid, with growing "power" segment demand tied to data-center generators. Exponential thesis watch

Compute: The Nvidia-SK Group $500bn LOI and Alphabet's reported "Frozen v2" custom TPU effort (targeting 6-10x tokens/watt by 2028) both point the same direction — every major compute buyer is now hedging against single-vendor dependence, whether through vertical integration (Google, Meta's "Iris" chips, Amazon's own silicon) or mega-scale supply locks (SK hynix HBM4, Micron's $22bn contracts). Azeem Azhar's 95%-undeployed-GPU data point is the counter-signal worth researching further — a gap between shipped and deployed compute would matter a great deal to anyone modeling near-term chip demand.

Energy: The grid, not capital or chips, is emerging as the AI buildout's real bottleneck — 14-year interconnection queues in Northern Virginia and roughly half of 2026's planned data centers at risk of delay. This is the through-line connecting Constellation's SMR investment (Blue Energy), GE Vernova's gas-turbine backlog, and the Trump administration's Saudi nuclear deal (however uncertain that agreement now looks) and its $200m SMR-acceleration initiative. Worth researching as candidates: other SMR developers and gas-turbine/grid-equipment suppliers beyond the names already held, given how tight turbine and switchgear capacity has become.

Manufacturing / robotics: Japan's ¥80 trillion national robotics push (targeting 10 million AI robots by 2040, with Toyota, FANUC, Kawasaki and others now in Nvidia's Cosmos Coalition) is a bigger, more concrete industrial-policy commitment than anything comparable in the US to date. Combined with Boston Dynamics' simplified new Atlas and Figure's BMW pilot, July 2026 looks like the moment humanoid robotics started producing real (if small) unit volumes rather than demos. Musk's own admission that Optimus's ramp will be "long and flat" given a near-total lack of a mature component supply chain is a useful reality check on how far the industrial base for humanoid robotics still has to go — that supply chain (actuators, sensors, specialized components) is itself a research candidate worth tracking as a picks-and-shovels angle, separate from the robot-makers themselves.

Biology: Isomorphic Labs' $2.1bn Series B (AI drug discovery, DeepMind spinout) was the largest single biotech financing tracked this cycle, alongside a broader rebound in biotech IPO activity. Merck KGaA's $11.3bn acquisition of Bio-Techne signals continued consolidation upstream of Thermo Fisher's own tools business — worth watching whether further M&A compresses TMO's competitive moat or simply validates the category's growth.
