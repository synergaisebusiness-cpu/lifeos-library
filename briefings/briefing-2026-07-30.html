Exponential thesis watch

Compute: The China DUV lithography story is the week's most important compute-pillar development — not because it changes anything today, but because it's the first concrete evidence (rather than rumor) that China's chip-equipment self-sufficiency timeline may be compressing faster than the market had priced. Worth researching further: how real and how close to leading-edge (vs. legacy-node) this DUV capacity actually is, since immersion DUV still trails ASML's EUV tools by a generation. On the efficiency side, Azeem Azhar's coverage of Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 (2.8T-parameter open MoE model) is a live example of the "compute efficiency frontier" counter-narrative to pure capex-scaling — the same tension visible in Alphabet's "Frozen v2" TPU effort and AMD/Micron's proprietary-supply-lock strategies noted in prior briefings.

Energy: PJM's decision to formally plan curtailment of large data centers from mid-2027 is the clearest evidence yet that the grid bottleneck is becoming an operational reality, not just a queueing statistic — and Baird's CAT downgrade this week shows the market is starting to price local/political backlash against data-center buildouts as a distinct risk, separate from raw grid capacity.
