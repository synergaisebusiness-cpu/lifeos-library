Macro backdrop

Iran sanctions: unveiled, and the market read it as a de-escalation of the disruption risk, not an escalation. Treasury Secretary Bessent formally unveiled the package Monday Aug 24 — internally dubbed "Operation Economic Outcast" — targeting a network of brokers, shipping/shadow-fleet operators, and crypto/gold/aviation channels moving Iranian oil revenue to the IRGC across the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore, Switzerland and Europe, alongside threats of secondary sanctions on countries (China, India, Turkey, Iraq, UAE flagged as most exposed) that don't cut economic ties to Tehran. The Washington Post characterized it as Bessent having "delayed the toughest blow." Oil, which had already been sliding into the announcement on profit-taking (Brent -1.3% to $93.17, WTI -1.4% to $85.86 on Aug 24), fell further once details landed — Brent to ~$89, WTI to ~$82 by Aug 25 — as traders concluded the measures were less likely to disrupt actual supply than the prior week's rhetoric implied. Iran's Finance Minister Ali Madanizadeh said Tehran is "fully prepared," citing a two-year contingency plan, called the move "economic terrorism," and vowed to respond "in a seismic manner." (NBC News, Washington Post, CNBC)

Sam Altman (OpenAI) — two substantive items. In a podcast recap published Aug 25, Altman argued AI adoption is being held back by economic inertia rather than the technology itself: *"We're still at the palm pilot stage of adoption. We have all of the technological pieces, but we have not had the iPhone moment,"* adding *"The economy just has so much inertia...
