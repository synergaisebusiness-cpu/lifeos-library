In an X thread posted Aug 15 (widely corroborated: TechCrunch, Fortune, Yahoo News UK, others, Aug 16-17), Amodei pushed back on claims his own messaging has disproportionately fueled AI backlash: "I think it is fundamentally a crisis of trust" — arguing "ordinary people don't trust companies, governments, or the tech industry" more broadly, not that AI messaging itself is the problem. He said "my messaging has been about equally balanced between risks and benefits," explaining he wrote "Machines of Loving Grace" precisely because the industry wasn't "painting an inspiring enough picture," and conceded: "By far the most accurate criticism of AI companies including Anthropic is that we haven't yet delivered on our big promises to benefit the world." On regulation, he pushed back on the claim that it entrenches incumbents, saying Anthropic makes "proposals that disadvantage frontier AI companies while advantaging smaller competitors," and that AI is "structurally a technology that tends to concentrate power." On medicine, he predicted AI could compress "100 years of medical progress in just 10" and that AI could eliminate most human illness within 5-10 years — framing actually curing diseases like cancer, not just promising to, as the real test of whether the industry earns public trust. (TechCrunch, Aug 16)
