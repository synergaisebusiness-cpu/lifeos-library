## Exponential thesis watch

**Compute**: The China DUV lithography story is the week's most important compute-pillar development — not because it changes anything today, but because it's the first concrete evidence (rather than rumor) that China's chip-equipment self-sufficiency timeline may be compressing faster than the market had priced. Worth researching further: how real and how close to leading-edge (vs. legacy-node) this DUV capacity actually is, since immersion DUV still trails ASML's EUV tools by a generation. On the efficiency side, Azeem Azhar's coverage of Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 (2.8T-parameter open MoE model) is a live example of the "compute efficiency frontier" counter-narrative to pure capex-scaling — the same tension visible in Alphabet's "Frozen v2" TPU effort and AMD/Micron's proprietary-supply-lock strategies noted in prior briefings.

**Energy**: PJM's decision to formally plan curtailment of large data centers from mid-2027 is the clearest evidence yet that the grid bottleneck is becoming an operational reality, not just a queueing statistic — and Baird's CAT downgrade this week shows the market is starting to price local/political backlash against data-center buildouts as a distinct risk, separate from raw grid capacity. The US-Saudi 123 agreement's confirmation (with a live 90-day Congressional review clock) and the Unity microreactor's criticality milestone at Idaho National Lab are both concrete steps forward for the nuclear/SMR leg of the energy thesis, worth tracking through Constellation's Aug 6 print.

**Manufacturing / robotics**: The Boston Dynamics acquisition closing alongside a live labor strike over Atlas deployment is a genuinely new wrinkle worth researching — humanoid robotics adoption may face organized labor resistance well before it faces technical or cost barriers in some markets (Korea specifically), which is a different risk profile than the "long and flat ramp" supply-chain story Musk has been describing for Optimus. Both are worth tracking as distinct constraints on the same thesis.

**Biology**: Scribe Therapeutics' gene-editing IPO (the first in over two years) and reports of a new AI-drug-discovery startup forming around ex-OpenAI researchers both point to continued capital and talent flow into the biology convergence pillar, building on the Isomorphic Labs and Bio-Techne activity already tracked. Worth researching as a candidate theme: which upstream tools/instrumentation providers (adjacent to TMO) stand to benefit as AI-native biotech startups scale their wet-lab needs.
