# Robotics & Quantum Computing — Investment Research

**Research date: 4 August 2026.** Public company research only; no personal positions or figures. Informational, not advice.

---

## Executive summary

**Robotics.** The industrial robot cycle is genuinely expanding — 621,000 installations globally in 2025, +15%, an all-time record. But the expansion is being driven by semiconductors, AI data centres and logistics, *not* by robots. Across essentially every listed "robotics" company, the division that is growing and carrying margin is the semicap or data-centre one, while robot-specific revenue is flat or margin-dilutive. **The robotics picks-and-shovels trade, as currently priced, is substantially an AI-semiconductor capex trade wearing a robotics costume.**

**Humanoids.** Roughly 18,000 units shipped globally in 2025, ~90% Chinese, and the overwhelming majority sold into research labs, universities and entertainment rather than productive work. Tesla Optimus has **zero disclosed production units**. **No Western listed company discloses any material humanoid revenue** — not "small", zero. Meanwhile the genuine chokepoint suppliers trade at 400x+ earnings.

**Quantum.** Six listed pure plays carry roughly **$43.9bn of market capitalisation on ~$232m of trailing revenue** — about 190x sales. Excluding IonQ (whose revenue is largely acquired), the other five carry $29.4bn on $44.9m: **655x sales**. Every one of them diluted shareholders 45–74% in the last twelve months. No commercially useful quantum advantage has been demonstrated. IBM's CEO — the most quantum-committed large-cap CEO — says measurable earnings impact arrives **2028–29**.

**The single most useful datapoint in this whole report** is in the quantum section: Oxford Instruments *sold* its dilution-refrigerator business — the purest listed quantum-enabler asset in the world — for £60m at roughly 1.0x sales and a 1.7% operating margin, at the exact moment its customers were being valued at 400–600x sales. The people who actually supply the picks and shovels looked at the economics and walked away.

**The Boston Dynamics valuation test.** Press and analyst chatter valued Boston Dynamics at $20bn, with talk of up to $100bn. On 16 July 2026 Hyundai bought SoftBank's remaining 9.9% for ~$325m — implying **~$3.3bn**. A ~6x gap between narrative and transacted value, on the most famous humanoid brand in the world, triggered by a put/call clause firing because the IPO didn't happen. Price every private humanoid mark accordingly.

**Forecast dispersion.** Goldman Sachs: >250,000 humanoid shipments in 2030, $38bn market by 2035. IFR — the trade body that physically counts robots — says **fewer than 100,000 globally by 2030**. A 2.5x spread between two credible institutions, with the people who count robots for a living being the most bearish. Note also that even Goldman's bullish 2035 humanoid number ($38bn) is *smaller than today's industrial robotics market* (~$54bn).

Bottom-up from actual disclosures, global humanoid revenue in 2025 was approximately **$0.6–1.0bn**. Figure AI's private valuation alone ($39bn) exceeds Goldman's entire 2035 global market forecast.

## 1.4 The actual bottleneck nobody prices as robotics: magnets

A humanoid needs ~28–40 actuator joints, each with a permanent-magnet motor. Optimus reportedly needs **~3.5kg of rare-earth magnets per unit**. At a one-million-unit run-rate that's ~3,500 tonnes/year of NdFeB — comparable to the *entire* combined nameplate capacity of MP Materials and Neo Performance. China holds ~85% of neodymium mine production and **~90% of global NdFeB magnet manufacture**.

| NdPr alloy price | |
|---|---|
| Jan 2026 | ~$53/kg |
| Jun 2026 | $109.55/kg |
| **3 Aug 2026** | **$133.67/kg — roughly +150% YTD** |

**Diary date: 10 November 2026.** China's suspended extraterritorial 0.1% de-minimis rare-earth rule snaps back that day unless re-suspended. The threshold applies at *component* level — a humanoid's actuator magnets blow through 0.1% trivially. If it reimposes, every non-Chinese robot manufacturer needs Chinese export licences for parts moving between two non-Chinese parties. This is the highest-impact binary date in the sector, and it is barely reflected in any "robotics" valuation.

Revenue *quality* is worse than the quantity. D-Wave's revenue went **backwards** ($24.6m FY2025 → $12.4m TTM). Rigetti's **fell 34%**. Quantinuum's Q1 2026 revenue fell **73% YoY**, with RIKEN alone 60% of 2025 revenue and government sources 71% of Q1 2026 revenue. QCi has $4.33m of revenue, a $1.83bn market cap, and faces securities-fraud class actions.

**Dilution is the business model.** These companies don't sell quantum computers at scale; they sell *shares*, using a high stock price as the raw material. A 50% annual share-count increase requires a 50% annual increase in enterprise value just to break even. You can pick the eventual winner and still lose money.

## 2.2 The technology

No commercially useful quantum advantage has been demonstrated. The two strongest 2026 claims:

- **IBM + Qedma (July 2026)** — a 2D Floquet Ising model on 133 qubits beating Fugaku-based classical methods, independently cross-checked on Quantinuum hardware. Real, and more than random-circuit sampling. But it used error *mitigation* (which scales exponentially) rather than correction, and nobody pays for a Floquet Ising answer.
- **Google Quantum Echoes (Oct 2025)** — closer to benchmark than application.

Logical-qubit leaderboards are misleading: QuEra's 96 logical qubits come from a high-rate, low-distance code, while Google's *single* logical qubit at 105 physical qubits was the genuinely hard result (first demonstration that logical error rate falls as code distance rises). Microsoft's topological Majorana claims remain contested, with a June 2026 analysis alleging basic errors in the underlying code.

IBM CEO Arvind Krishna, 30 July 2026: measurable earnings impact **2028 or 2029**, fault tolerance targeted 2029. Total *global industry* revenue in 2025 was ~$1bn (McKinsey) — the entire worldwide quantum industry earns about what a mid-cap software company does.

## 2.3 The datapoint that undermines the enabler thesis

In 2025 **Oxford Instruments sold NanoScience** — its dilution-refrigerator business, without which superconducting quantum computers cannot physically operate — to Quantum Design for **£60m**. That unit had FY2025 revenue of **£59m and adjusted operating profit of £1m**: a 1.7% operating margin, sold at roughly **1.0x sales**, at the exact moment its customers were being valued at 400–600x sales.

And the rest of the layer isn't listed anyway: **Bluefors** (refrigerator market leader) is private; **Zurich Instruments** (control electronics) was acquired by Rohde & Schwarz; **Quantum Design** is private. Every pure-play quantum enabler is private, was acquired, or was divested by its listed parent.

The listed "picks and shovels" — Coherent, Keysight, MKS — are genuinely good businesses whose quantum exposure is a rounding error and is not separately disclosed by any of them. Own them for AI optics, test equipment and semicap. Don't tell yourself you're buying quantum.

**The one exception worth knowing:** Honeywell's ~48% of Quantinuum is now roughly **9% of Honeywell's market cap** — the only material, non-diluting quantum stake attached to a business earning $8.2bn.

# PART 5 — CONCLUSIONS

**On robotics.** The theme is real and the cycle is genuinely turning up. But three things are true simultaneously: the growth is being driven by semiconductors and logistics rather than robots; humanoid revenue at listed Western companies is *zero*; and the genuine chokepoint assets trade at 400x+ earnings with deteriorating earnings and visible Chinese share loss. The names where fundamentals and price are both defensible are the *boring* ones — Nabtesco, Cognex, Keyence, Novanta, Allegro, Renishaw, Fanuc — and several of them are, on the numbers, currently AI-capex businesses.

The binding physical constraint on humanoid scale is rare-earth magnets, not AI. That constraint is priced as a China-policy commodity trade, not as robotics.

**On quantum.** The science is genuinely exciting and genuinely early. The securities are genuinely expensive and genuinely dilutive. Those are separate facts and conflating them is the entire investment error. At 190x aggregate sales with 45–74% annual dilution, no commercial revenue base, and the most committed CEO in the industry pointing at 2028–29, the honest position is that this is not yet investable. The historical base rate for retail buying pre-revenue deep tech at a hype peak — fuel cells 2000, 3D printing 2013, cannabis 2018, EV SPACs 2021 — is an 80–95% drawdown *even when the technology eventually works*. Cisco's internet was real too.

**On thematic ETFs.** Five years of data, and every robotics fund lost to a plain world tracker. The ones that kept up did so by being semiconductor funds in disguise. If the conclusion is "I want exposure to this theme," the uncomfortable finding is that a global index fund has delivered the theme's returns better than the theme's funds have.
