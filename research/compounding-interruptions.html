The cost of interrupting compounding

Your plan's contribution ladder run from 22 to 60 in today's money, through the same market (≈7.5%/yr nominal with a −25% crash every 8 years, ≈4.4%/yr real). Chart 1 varies behaviour; chart 2 varies the career path after 30 with behaviour held perfect. The market is identical in every line.

Assumptions. Contributions (ISA + pension, nominal): £17.1k at 22 stepping with the plan's salary ladder to ~£40k/yr at £80k salary and ~£50k/yr at ~£100k TC from 29; start pot £14k (Sept-2026 trough); crashes at 26, 34, 42, 50; values deflated at 3% to today's purchasing power. Behaviours (chart 1): panic pauser stops contributing for three years from each crash (skipping exactly the cheapest buys); house raid withdraws £80k at 30 and halves contributions for three years — that money becomes home equity, not smoke, so its true cost is smaller than the line suggests; kids downshift halves contributions 33–39; lifestyle creep never captures raises (contributions grow with inflation only). Career paths (chart 2, all unbroken behaviour): flat = real contributions constant from 29; steady climb = +3%/yr real from 29; US-comp jump = real contributions double from 32; coast = contributions stop entirely at 40; max ISA from 22 = £20k/yr ISA + full pension from age 22 (adds ~£50k over ages 22–26 vs salary alone — this line assumes the Synergaise bridge delivers ~£12–13k/yr net in those years, since salary-only cash capacity is £6.4–7.6k/yr). Illustrative model, not a forecast — real markets don't crash on a timetable, and sequence risk can be kinder or crueller than this.
