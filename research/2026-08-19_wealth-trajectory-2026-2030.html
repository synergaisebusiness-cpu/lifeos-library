Five things worth reading carefully

1 — The two-year number barely moves with pay rises

Floor and Base are identical at £56,172, because your own ladder puts the first rise (£52k at age 24) right at the two-year boundary. Rises matter enormously in years 3–4 and almost not at all in years 1–2.

So: over 24 months you're looking at roughly £56k almost regardless of what happens with your salary. That's your discipline, not your career.

2 — Nearly half of it is money you can't touch

At four years, base case: £118,096 total, but £52,552 of that is pension — locked until 57+. Accessible is £65,544.

The pension line is the fastest-growing thing you own and it's the right call at a 15% employer match. Just don't read £118k as £118k of options. The number that buys you a house, a career break or a move abroad is the £65,544.

3 — Capturing the raise is worth £15,317 over four years

Same salary ladder, three behaviours:

Over four years that's a bad-but-survivable gap. Your own compounding research put the lifetime cost of never capturing raises at −£2.04m. This table is the first four years of that curve, which is why it looks small here and doesn't stay small.

4 — Living costs will not stay flat

You asked me to hold them constant, so I did. But your tenancy allows s13 rent reviews, and bills, food and council tax all move. At +3%/yr — modest — the four-year total drops from £118,096 to £112,794. Roughly £5,302, and it's the realistic case, not the pessimistic one.

Rent is the one to watch: other 3-beds in the building were advertised from £4,385 against your £4,685, which is your evidence at the first review.

5 — The ISA allowance never becomes your constraint

Even at £65k with costs flat, your surplus peaks around £16,476/yr against a £20,000 allowance. You never fill it on salary alone, so no GIA is ever needed.

That's the same finding as this morning, at a longer horizon: the wrapper isn't the limit, income is. The £20k cap only starts to bite if Synergaise works.
