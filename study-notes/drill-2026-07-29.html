Part A — Maths placement (16:00, 30 min)

The job: find your true starting rung. Not to score well.

Go to khanacademy.org, make an account, then: Math → Algebra 1 → Course challenge. That's Khan's own placement test. Give it about 20 minutes.

Rules for this one only: no calculator, and if you don't know, skip rather than guess. A lucky guess places you a rung too high and you'll feel it for a fortnight.

Report back three things: your score, the topic names Khan marked as needing work, and any question where you understood the words but had no idea what to do. That third one is the most useful of the three.

If Algebra 1 feels like it's coming at you too fast, drop to Algebra basics → Course challenge and say so. Starting lower costs nothing. Starting too high costs weeks.

Part B — SQL L0, first contact (16:30, 30 min)

Setup (~10 min, once)

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com/bigquery and sign in with a Google account.
  2. Accept the terms. You'll land in the sandbox — free, and it does not ask for a card. If it asks for billing details, you've gone down the wrong path; stop and tell me.
  3. Create a project when prompted. Call it anything — lloyds-prep is fine.
  4. In the left Explorer panel, click + Add → Public datasets → search thelook_ecommerce → add it. It'll appear under bigquery-public-data.

Before you write any SQL

Expand thelook_ecommerce and look at the tables — users, products, orders, order_items and a few more. Click on products, then the Schema tab.

Read the column names and their types. That's it, for two full minutes. You're doing the thing your notes described this morning from the other side: here the column carries the type, declared up front, enforced by the database. Same idea, opposite arrangement to Python.

Then click the users table and read its schema too. You'll need both.

The five questions

Answer these in SQL. Only SELECT, FROM, WHERE, ORDER BY and LIMIT — no GROUP BY, no JOIN, those are later rungs. You find the column names yourself from the schema. That's part of the drill, not an obstacle to it.

  1. Show the name and retail price of any 10 products.
  2. Show the name and price of every product costing more than 100.
  3. Show the 10 most expensive products, most expensive first.
  4. Show the first name, last name and city of users in the United Kingdom.
  5. Show the 5 cheapest products in the Jeans category.

Write the English question in your notes first, then the SQL underneath it. Every time. Those pairs are not busywork — they become the golden set your capstone gets evaluated against in August, so the drilling and the deliverable are literally the same work.

One habit to start today

Before you hit Run, look at the top-right of the editor: BigQuery tells you how much data the query will process. Glance at it every single time, from now on.

thelook_ecommerce is tiny and the sandbox gives you 1 TB a month, so nothing you do today can cost anything. The point is the reflex. Cost awareness is explicitly in the NPD job description under "trade-offs", and a graduate who checks the estimate before running is already behaving like someone who's worked with production data.

Paste the queries and what came back — including anything that errored — and I'll mark them line by line. Five correct answers is the L0 checkpoint, but that's not until 3 August; today is first contact, so getting stuck is the expected outcome, not a failure.
