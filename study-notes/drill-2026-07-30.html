The six

1 · Logs Solve log₂(x) = 5.
Then simplify log(a) + log(b) − log(c) into a single log.
2 · Quadratics Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
3 · Composite functions f(x) = 2x + 1 and g(x) = x².
Find f(g(3)), then g(f(3)). Say in one line why they differ.
4 · Straight lines Find the equation of the line through (1, 4) and (3, 10).
5 · Differentiation Differentiate y = 3x² + 5x − 2.
Then differentiate y = (2x + 1)⁵.
6 · Stats, in words What is the difference between the mean and the median, and give one concrete situation where reporting the mean would mislead someone?

Two things from last night worth having to hand:

  • Three-part names. Public data lives in someone else's project, so every table is project.dataset.table — here bigquery-public-data.thelook_ecommerce.products.
  • Reading a schema without hunting the UI: 
    SELECT column_name, data_type
FROM `bigquery-public-data.thelook_ecommerce.INFORMATION_SCHEMA.COLUMNS`
WHERE table_name = 'products';
    Swap 'products' for any table name. Faster than clicking, and it works when the Explorer panel doesn't.

The five questions (carried from 29 July)

Only SELECT, FROM, WHERE, ORDER BY and LIMIT. No GROUP BY, no JOIN — later rungs. Find the column names yourself; that's part of the drill.

  1. Show the name and retail price of any 10 products.
  2. Show the name and price of every product costing more than 100.
  3. Show the 10 most expensive products, most expensive first.
  4. Show the first name, last name and city of users in the United Kingdom.
  5. Show the 5 cheapest products in the Jeans category.

Write the English question in your notes first, then the SQL underneath it. Every time. Those pairs become the golden set your capstone gets evaluated against in August — the drilling and the deliverable are the same work.
