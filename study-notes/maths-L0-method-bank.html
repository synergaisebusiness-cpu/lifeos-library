1. Linear equations — incl. fractions and decimals

Undo what was done to x, one operation per line, always to both sides. Fractions: multiply them away first. Decimals: multiply by 10 or 100 first.

Worked: 3x + 7 = 25

3x + 7 = 25x was ×3'd then +7'd — undo in reverse order
3x = 18subtract 7 from both sides
x = 6divide both sides by 3

Worked, with a fraction: (2x − 5) / 3 = 4

(2x − 5) / 3 = 4the whole left side is divided by 3 — kill that first
2x − 5 = 12multiply both sides by 3
2x = 17add 5 to both sides
x = 8.5divide by 2 — non-whole answers are fine; don't distrust them

Worked, x on both sides: 5x − 4 = 2x + 11

5x − 4 = 2x + 11collect x on the side that has more of them
3x − 4 = 11subtract 2x from both sides
3x = 15add 4
x = 5divide by 3

Worked, decimals: 0.4x + 1.2 = 3.6

4x + 12 = 36multiplied every term by 10 — decimals gone, same equation
4x = 24subtract 12
x = 6divide by 4
Reps: (a) 4x − 9 = 19 (b) (3x + 1)/2 = 8 (c) 7x + 3 = 4x + 18 (d) 0.3x − 0.6 = 1.5

2. Expanding brackets — especially over a minus

The thing outside multiplies everything inside. A minus outside flips every sign inside.

Worked: 3(2x + 5)

3(2x + 5)3 hits both terms
6x + 153·2x and 3·5

Worked, the dangerous one: 7 − 2(x − 4)

7 − 2(x − 4)what's outside the bracket is −2, not 2
7 − 2x + 8−2·x = −2x, and −2·(−4) = +8 — the sign flips
15 − 2xcollect the numbers
The trap: writing 7 − 2x − 8. If you multiplied −2 by −4 and got −8, you multiplied by 2 and stuck the minus on afterwards. The minus is part of the number.
Reps: (a) 5(3x − 2) (b) 10 − 3(x − 5) (c) 4(2x + 1) − 2(x − 3)

Double brackets — (x+3)(x+2) — are deliberately not here. That's quadratics, taught 7 Aug.

3. Change of subject

Exactly the same game as section 1, except the answer is a letter. Undo everything that's been done to the letter you want, in reverse order.

Worked: v = u + at, make t the subject

v = u + att was ×a'd, then +u'd — undo in reverse
v − u = atsubtract u from both sides
t = (v − u) / adivide both sides by a — bracket the whole top

Worked, multi-step: v² = u² + 2as, make u the subject

v² = u² + 2asu was squared, then had 2as added
v² − 2as = u²subtract 2as from both sides
u = √(v² − 2as)square root both sides — root the whole expression, hence the bracket

Worked, subject on the bottom: s = d / t, make t the subject

s = d / tt is trapped in a denominator — first move: multiply it out
st = dmultiply both sides by t
t = d / sdivide both sides by s
Reps: (a) A = lw, make w the subject (b) y = mx + c, make x the subject (c) E = ½mv², make v the subject (d) P = F/A, make A the subject

4. Dividing by a fraction

Dividing by a fraction = multiplying by it flipped.

Worked: 6 ÷ ¾

6 ÷ 3/4the question means: how many ¾'s fit in 6?
6 × 4/3flip the fraction, switch to multiply
24/3 = 8and sense-check: ¾ fits into 6 more than 6 times — 8 is plausible

Worked, inside an equation: (2/3)x = 8

(2/3)x = 8x was multiplied by ⅔ — undo by dividing by ⅔
x = 8 × 3/2dividing by ⅔ = multiplying by 3/2
x = 12check: ⅔ of 12 is 8 ✓
Reps: (a) 10 ÷ ⅖ (b) (3/4)x = 9 (c) ½ ÷ ¼

5. English → algebra

Name the unknown in writing. Translate phrase by phrase, not sentence by sentence. Sanity-check the finished equation with a small number before solving it.

Worked: "A gym charges £20 to join and £3 per visit. After how many visits have you spent £50?"

let v = number of visitsalways written down — the unnamed unknown is where it goes wrong
cost = 20 + 3v"£20 to join" = one-off, "£3 per visit" = 3 × v
20 + 3v = 50"spent £50" pins the total
3v = 30 → v = 10section 1 takes over from here

Worked: "Three consecutive numbers add up to 45."

let n = the smallestconsecutive = each one is 1 more than the last
n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 45the three numbers, written in terms of one unknown
3n + 3 = 45collect terms
n = 14 → 14, 15, 16check: 14+15+16 = 45 ✓ — always answer the actual question, not just "n = 14"
Reps: (a) "A number doubled, then reduced by 7, gives 31 — find it." (b) "A taxi charges £4 plus £2 per mile. A ride costs £18 — how many miles?"

6. Functions and f(x) notation

f(x) is a machine with a rule inside. f(anything) means: substitute that thing everywhere x appears — numbers, negatives, even expressions.

Worked: f(x) = 3x − 2

f(4) = 3·4 − 2 = 10x becomes 4, everywhere
f(−1) = 3·(−1) − 2 = −5negatives go in with their sign attached
f(2a) = 3·(2a) − 2 = 6a − 2expressions substitute exactly the same way — bracket them going in

Worked, solving backwards: f(x) = 13, find x

3x − 2 = 13"f(x) = 13" just means the machine's output is 13 — write the rule equal to it
x = 5section 1 again

You already know this machine from the other ladder: def f(x): return 3*x - 2. Same object, older notation. Composite functions — f(g(x)), machines feeding machines — the probe scored gone, so they get taught properly, not bank-carded here.

Reps, with g(x) = 2x + 5: (a) g(3) (b) g(−4) (c) g(x) = 17, find x (d) g(3b)

7. Straight lines — gradient and intercept

y = mx + c. m = gradient = rise over run = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). c = where it crosses the y-axis (the value of y when x = 0).

Worked: the line through (1, 5) and (3, 11)

m = (11 − 5) / (3 − 1)rise over run — keep both subtractions in the same order
m = 6/2 = 3for every 1 across, the line goes 3 up
y = 3x + cgradient in; c still unknown
5 = 3·1 + cfeed in a point the line definitely passes through — (1, 5)
c = 2 → y = 3x + 2check with the other point: 3·3 + 2 = 11 ✓
The trap: mixing the order — (11 − 5)/(1 − 3) gives m = −3, a line sloping the wrong way. Pick which point is "point 2" and stay loyal to it in both the top and the bottom.

Worth knowing where this is going: the gradient — "how fast y changes as x moves" — is the exact object the derivative generalises on 24 Aug. You are not learning this twice; you're learning it once, early.

Reps: (a) gradient of the line through (2, 3) and (6, 15) (b) full equation of the line through (0, 4) and (2, 10) (c) for y = −2x + 7: gradient? y-intercept?

8. Exponents and index laws — today

Three core laws: multiply → add the powers · divide → subtract · power of a power → multiply. Everything else in this section is a consequence of those three.

8.1 Multiplying powers: x³ · x⁴

x³ · x⁴write it out once, ever: (x·x·x)·(x·x·x·x)
x⁷seven x's multiplied — so the powers add: 3 + 4

8.2 Dividing powers: y⁷ ÷ y²

y⁷ / y²seven y's on top, two on the bottom
y⁵two cancel — powers subtract: 7 − 2

8.3 Power of a power: (a²)⁵

(a²)⁵a² written down five times: a²·a²·a²·a²·a²
a¹⁰adding 2 five times = multiplying: 2 × 5

8.4 Power zero: 5⁰

5³ / 5³ = 1anything divided by itself is 1 — no exponent knowledge needed
5³ / 5³ = 5⁰but by law 8.2 it's also 5³⁻³
5⁰ = 1both must be true — so power zero is 1, always, for anything

8.5 Negative power: 3⁻²

3¹ / 3³ = 3⁻²law 8.2: 1 − 3 = −2
3 / (3·3·3) = 1/9the same thing written out and cancelled
3⁻² = 1/3² = 1/9negative power = flip it, then the power is positive

8.6 Power over brackets: (2x)³

(2x)³ = 2x · 2x · 2xthe whole bracket is cubed
8x³everything inside gets the power — including the 2
(x/y)³ = x³/y³same rule on a fraction: top and bottom both get it
The trap: (2x)³ = 2x³. The 2 escaped the cube. It shouldn't have — the bracket says the power applies to all of it. 2x³ and 8x³ differ by a factor of four.

8.7 Fractional power: it's a root

9^½ · 9^½ = 9¹law 8.1: ½ + ½ = 1 — so 9^½ times itself is 9
9^½ = √9 = 3"times itself gives 9" is the definition of the square root
8^⅓ = ∛8 = 2same logic: three of them multiply to 8
16^¾ = (16^¼)³ = 2³ = 8law 8.3 in reverse: ¾ = ¼ × 3 — root first (small numbers), then power

8.8 Mixed: (2a²b)³ ÷ 4a³b²

(2a²b)³ = 8a⁶b³law 8.6 on the bracket: 2³, (a²)³ by law 8.3, b³
8a⁶b³ / 4a³b²now it's just division, piece by piece
2a³b8÷4 = 2, a⁶⁻³ = a³, b³⁻² = b¹ — law 8.2 three times
