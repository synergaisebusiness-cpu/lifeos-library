### The lid-knob trap
Cheap cast-iron casseroles ship with phenolic knobs rated 180–200°C. Sourdough bakes at 230–250°C.
Either buy one with a stainless knob, or unscrew it and fit a stainless bolt + washer (~£1, any
hardware shop). Two minutes; removes the only real failure point on a £30 pot.

### The £0 fallbacks
- **Pot:** a stainless stockpot with an oven-safe lid (no plastic anywhere), or a deep roasting tin
  with a second tin inverted over it for the first 20 min. Worse seal, less trapped steam, slightly
  less spring — still real bread.
- **Banneton:** mixing bowls lined with floured tea towels. You lose the spiral rings on the crust.
  That's the entire difference.

## Three decisions now in diet.md

**1. Bake it 30–50% wholemeal.** You're right that 3-ingredient sourdough is better food — no DATEM,
no calcium propionate, no added sugar, no seed oil, and the long ferment lowers phytates so you
actually absorb the zinc and magnesium. And at £3.50/month split three ways it passes the per-£ half
of your standing rule by a mile. The one axis it's weak on is per-calorie density, and wholemeal
closes most of that gap for free:

| Per 200 kcal | Weight | Fibre | Potassium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boiled potato | ~260g | 5.7g | ~1,100mg |
| Wholemeal sourdough | ~83g | ~5g | Low |
| White sourdough | ~77g | 1.8g | Low |

**2. Slice, weigh, freeze — from loaf one.** diet.md names untracked extras as your weak point, and a
warm communal loaf on a shared counter is the most efficient untracked-calorie machine you could
install in that flat. Two unlogged slices is ~200 kcal, 10% of target, invisible. A frozen portioned
slice is a decision; a loaf under a tea towel isn't. This is why items 7 and 8 are on the list.

**3. Protein bread waits.** 10% vital wheat gluten gets you ~13–14g protein/100g — a real
improvement, and worth doing eventually. But it's **~5.4g protein per 100 kcal vs quark's ~17.6**, so
it's a better bread, not a protein source; don't let it into the 198g/day maths. More importantly:
your own one-variable-at-a-time rule. Adding sourdough, flat-wide baking *and* a concentrated gluten
load in the same fortnight — while gluten/fibre clustering sits at #1 on your bloat suspect ladder —
means you can't attribute anything. Plain wholemeal for 2–3 weeks, then decide.
