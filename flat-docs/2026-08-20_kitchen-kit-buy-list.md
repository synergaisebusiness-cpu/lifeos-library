## Block A — meal prep (diet.md launch kit)

### 1. Overnight-oats jars ×7 — **750ml, not 500ml**
`£15–20 for 6` · or **£0**

⚠️ **diet.md says 500ml. That's too small.** OO v2 is oats 60g (~120ml dry) + Greek yogurt 150g +
quark 100g + milk 50ml + chia 10g + PB 15g + berries 80g. That's ~465g and roughly 500–550ml

- **Buy:** "750ml glass clip top jars set of 6" — Kilner / Le Parfait / generic. ~£15–20.
- **Cheaper:** IKEA KORKEN 1L clip jars, ~£3 each.
- **Free:** saved passata and peanut-butter jars. diet.md already flags this — if you've got 7
  empties, skip this line.

*(Buy only if oats stay — see the open question at the bottom.)*

### 2. Glass meal-prep containers ×7
`£25–32`

- **Spec:** 800ml–1L rectangular, borosilicate glass, snap-lock lid with silicone seal.
- **Why 800ml+:** D2 (turkey chilli + potato) is 636 kcal — a bulky portion. 700ml sets are a squeeze.
- **Search:** "glass meal prep containers set of 7 leakproof". Prep Naturals, Igluu, Bayco, M MCIRCO.
- Glass bases are oven-safe, lids never are. Fine for microwave reheating either way.

### 3. Digital kitchen scale
`£8–12`

Non-negotiable in diet.md, and doubly so now — sourdough is a baker's-percentage system, and a
communal loaf only stays inside your macros if slices get weighed.

- **Spec:** 5kg capacity · **1g resolution** · tare button · platform wide enough for a mixing bowl.
- Don't pay extra for 0.1g. Do check it takes AA/AAA, not an obscure coin cell.
- Amazon Basics or Salter both fine.

## Block B — sourdough (revised for flat-scale baking)

### 4. Stainless mixing bowls — **go 6–7L on the big one**
`£15–20`

Revised up from 5L. A 1kg-flour double batch is ~1.7kg of dough and roughly doubles in bulk
ferment — 5L is borderline, 6–7L is comfortable.

- Buy a **nested set of 3** (~7L / 3L / 1.5L). Same money as one good large bowl, and you need a
  second for dry mix / levain anyway.
- The big one also doubles as a free banneton (item 6).

### 5. Baking vessel — "cheapest that works"
`£28–40` · or **£0**

**The spec that actually matters:**

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| Cast iron, lidded, 24–26cm / 4–5L | Holds heat when the oven door opens; room for oven spring |
| **Oven-safe to 250°C — lid knob included** | ⚠️ **The trap.** Cheap phenolic/bakelite knobs are rated 180–200°C and scorch or melt at sourdough temps |
| No PTFE / non-stick coating | Coating degrades above ~230°C |

- **Buy:** search "cast iron casserole dish with lid 4.5L". Generic enamelled ~**£28–40**;
  Amazon Basics enamelled ~£35–45.
- **Knob fix:** if the pot you want has a plastic knob, unscrew it and fit a stainless bolt and
  washer (~£1, any hardware shop). Two minutes, removes the only failure point.
- **The £0 option:** a stainless stockpot with an oven-safe lid (no plastic anywhere), or a deep
  roasting tin with a second tin inverted over it for the first 20 minutes. Worse seal, less trapped
  steam, slightly less spring — but it bakes real bread.

⚠️ **Flat-scale note:** one pot = one loaf at a time. Two loaves is two sequential bakes, ~2 hours of
oven. For three people that's the realistic Sunday rhythm — bake two, slice, freeze. Don't buy a
second pot yet; see whether the habit sticks first.

**Why the lid matters:** you cover the loaf for the first ~20 min so the dough's own moisture stays
trapped and the crust stays soft while it springs, then uncover to colour it. Anything lidded and
oven-safe does that; cast iron just does it better.

### 6. Banneton (proofing basket) — **×2 if baking doubles**
`£10–14 each` · or **£0**

- 25cm oval or 22–23cm round rattan, with linen liner.
- Two loaves proof *simultaneously*, so a double batch needs two baskets (unlike the pot, which
  can go sequentially).
- **Free alternative:** mixing bowls lined with clean tea towels, floured heavily. Works properly.
  You lose the spiral rings on the crust — that's the whole difference.

### 7. Scrapers
`£8 for both`

- **Metal bench scraper (~£5)** — dividing, shaping, clearing the counter. Most underrated tool here.
- **Flexible plastic bowl scraper (~£3)** — gets dough out of the bowl cleanly.

### 8. Lame (scoring blade)
`£6–8` · or `£3`

A dedicated lame, or **double-edged razor blades (~£3 for 10)** taped to a coffee stirrer. A kitchen
knife does **not** work — it drags the skin instead of slicing, and the loaf tears instead of opening.

### 9. 🆕 Bread knife
`£8–12`

Missing from v1 and genuinely needed now you're slicing 2 loaves a week for three people. Serrated,
**20cm+ blade**, offset handle if you can. A short serrated knife mangles a 25cm boule.

### 10. 🆕 Freezer bags or slice containers
`£4–6`

This is the *tracking* mechanism, not just storage — see the risk note below. Slice the whole loaf
on bake day, weigh, portion, freeze. Toast straight from frozen.

### 11. 🆕 Vital wheat gluten — only if you go protein bread
`£8–10 / 1kg` · **Amazon is the right place** (supermarkets don't stock it)

The actual lever for protein bread. ~75–80% protein. See the honest maths below before committing.

### 12. Baking parchment
`~£2 — supermarket`

For the sling that lowers the loaf into a 250°C pot. Non-optional unless you like burns.

### 💡 Bundle tip
Amazon UK sells "sourdough starter kits" bundling **banneton + liner + lame + blades + bench scraper
+ dough scraper + dough whisk** for ~£20–25 — cheaper than items 6–8 separately (~£25–30). You have
the jar, so skip kits priced up around a fancy starter jar. For two bannetons, buy the bundle plus
one loose basket.

### Later, not now
- **Instant-read thermometer (~£10)** — dough temp matters more than beginners admit; fermentation
  rate roughly doubles per 10°C. Buy once you want repeatability.
- **Cooling rack (~£8)** — bread must cool on a rack or the base steams itself soggy.
- **Ninja Creami** — October, per flat.md §9. Split with Kaleb, your share ~£65–85. October close is
  projected ~£9,061, so it's affordable then and not now.

## Flour — supermarket, not Amazon (except the gluten)

At 2 loaves/week for the flat you're using ~1kg flour/week, ~4kg/month.

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Strong white bread flour, 1.5kg | £1.30–2 | Own-brand is fine. **~£3.50/month** at flat volume |
| **Wholemeal or dark rye, 1kg** | £1.50 | Feed the starter with this — noticeably more active. **Also see the 30–50% wholemeal recommendation below** |
| Rice flour, small bag | £1.50 | **Anti-stick trick.** Dust the banneton with it — doesn't absorb moisture, so it doesn't glue the loaf to the basket the way wheat flour does |
| Vital wheat gluten, 1kg | £8–10 | Amazon only. Optional |

**On your value rule:** flour is one of the cheapest calorie sources that exists. At ~£3.50/month
split three ways, sourdough passes the "per £" half of your standing rule by a mile. The "per
calorie" half is where it's weaker — see below.

## The honest read on potatoes + sourdough as your carb rails

### Potatoes — you're completely right, no argument
Boiled potato is ~77 kcal/100g, tops the satiety index, and is the **#1 potassium source per £ and
per calorie** in your own debloat work. Making it a primary carb rail is straightforwardly correct
and already what diet.md says.

### Sourdough — good, but it's the least calorie-effective carb you'd have
Your standing rule is "max protein / fat / fibre / micronutrients **per calorie** AND per £." Here's
where bread actually lands, per **200 kcal**:

| | Weight for 200 kcal | Protein | Fibre | Potassium | Volume/satiety |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Boiled potato** | ~260g | 5.7g | 5.7g | ~1,100mg | Enormous |
| **White sourdough** | ~77g (2 slices) | ~7g | ~1.8g | Low | Small |
| **Wholemeal sourdough** | ~83g | ~8.3g | ~5g | Low | Small |

Bread isn't unhealthy — it's *dense*. 260g of potato and 2 slices of bread are the same calories and
nowhere near the same meal. That's the whole tension, and it's manageable rather than disqualifying.

**→ The specific fix: bake at 30–50% wholemeal.** It nearly closes the fibre gap, adds protein,
ferments faster, and makes a better starter. White sourdough is the version that genuinely underperforms.

### "Protein bread" — a better bread, not a protein source
Add 10% vital wheat gluten and a 1kg-flour loaf goes from ~12g to **~13–14g protein/100g**. That's a
real improvement. But per calorie:

- Protein bread: **~5.4g protein per 100 kcal**
- Quark: ~17.6g · Chicken breast: ~18.8g

**Roughly 3.5× worse than your existing protein anchors.** So: make protein bread if you enjoy it,
but don't let it displace quark, chicken or eggs in the 198g/day maths. It's a carb that carries
some protein, not a protein source.

⚠️ **And sequence it.** Your own operating rule in diet.md is **one variable at a time** so results
can be attributed. Right now you'd be adding sourdough, flat-wide baking, *and* a concentrated
gluten load all in the same week — while gluten/fibre clustering sits at #1 on your gut-bloat suspect
list. **Plain 30–50% wholemeal sourdough first.** Establish whether it's bloat-neutral over 2–3 weeks,
*then* add the gluten if you still want it. Don't buy item 11 yet.

### 🎯 The real risk isn't the macros — it's the communal loaf
diet.md is explicit that your weak point is **adherence: untracked extras and weekend drift**. A warm
loaf on a shared counter that three people pick at is the single most efficient untracked-calorie
machine you could install in that flat. Two slices you didn't log is ~200 kcal — 10% of your daily
target, invisible.

**This is solvable and cheap:** slice the whole loaf on bake day, weigh it once, portion and freeze
(items 9 + 10). A frozen portioned slice is a decision; a loaf under a tea towel is not. Do this from
loaf one, not after you notice the drift.

### One genuine point in its favour
Long fermentation breaks down fructans — **#4 on your gut-bloat suspect list**. If bread currently
bloats you, sourdough is plausibly the version that doesn't. Worth logging in hypotheses.md as a
dated, low-confidence hypothesis and actually watching, rather than assuming either way.
