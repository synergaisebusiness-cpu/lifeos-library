How much buffer — the honest answer

First, a correction to how you're reading your own balance

The ~£3,150 settling-in payment arriving ~20 Oct is repayable in full if you leave Lloyds within 12 months. Until roughly October 2027 it is a conditional asset sitting inside your buffer. If you count it, you look like you hit £9k in October. If you don't, your real buffer at end of March is £6,529, not £9,679.

That matters because the whole point of a buffer is that it works in exactly the scenario — losing or leaving the job — where this £3,150 evaporates. A buffer that shrinks when you need it isn't a buffer.

Two things I found in the pension numbers

The felt cost in wealth-plan.md is wrong

The file says the 6% sacrifice has a £153/mo felt cost. Recomputed at 2026/27 rates it's £162/mo — the £153 figure assumes 12% employee NI, but basic-rate NI is 8%. Small, but it's a source-of-truth file, so it's now corrected.

The headline still holds and it's the best line in your whole plan: £162 of net pay buys £787.50 of pension. 4.86×. Nothing else you can do with £162 comes close.

You're already over the reported 2029 sacrifice cap

Your file notes a reported £2,000/yr NI cap on salary sacrifice from 2029. Your 6% is £2,700/yr — already £700 over it. So if that rule lands as described, part of your sacrifice starts attracting NI in 2029, and the Layer-3 "£1 sacrificed = £1.87" maths needs redoing before it goes live.

Not urgent — three years out and the detail still needs verifying. But it stops being theoretical the moment you consider raising contributions above 6%.
