**24 Aug 2026 — companion to the Week B shop list**

without giving yourself botulism, because that's a real risk here and not a theoretical one.

## Part 2 — The egg jar

Yes, this works, and it's a good idea: eggs are already snack P3 (2 eggs = 144 kcal / 13 g P), a
vinegar brine adds **effectively zero calories**, and it turns a boring snack into one you'll
actually reach for. Cook a dozen once, eat them for weeks.

But two things have to be said before the recipe.

### ⚠️ Read this bit properly

**Pickled eggs live in the fridge. Always. Not the counter, not a shelf, not "it's got vinegar in it".**

The reason is specific and worth understanding: the *brine* being acidic does not acidify the
*inside of the egg*. Egg yolk sits at pH ~6.8 — a perfect low-acid, anaerobic pocket for
*Clostridium botulinum*. In the documented 1997 Illinois case, a man hard-boiled and peeled eggs,
**punctured them with toothpicks**, jarred them with beets and hot peppers, and left the jar at
room temperature in the sun. The brine tested at pH 3.5 — perfectly safe on paper. He was
hospitalised with type B botulism: double vision, unable to speak, difficulty breathing. The
piercing carried spores into the yolk, and the yolk was never acidified.

So, the rules:
1. **Fridge at all times.** Out only for serving, and under 2 hours.
2. **Never pierce the eggs** to speed up seasoning. That is exactly the failure mode.
3. **There is no home-canning process for pickled eggs.** Don't water-bath them. Nobody has a
   tested one. Fridge is the only method.
4. Clean jar, brine poured on hot, into the fridge immediately.
5. **Use within 3–4 months.**

### The design that actually fits your week

Here's the catch nobody mentions: **large eggs take 2–4 weeks to season.** So this isn't a
Sunday-prep-for-Monday thing. Run **two jars offset by two weeks** — jar A goes in today, jar B in
a fortnight, and from then on you always have one ready while the other matures. That's the whole
system.

**Base brine (one ~1 L jar, ~12 eggs):**
- 1½ cups (~350 ml) white or cider vinegar
- 1 cup (~240 ml) water
- **1 tsp salt** — the NCHFP recipes use 3 tsp; cut it. The vinegar does the preserving, the salt
  is only flavour, and sodium is a named watch-point in your debloat focus
- 1 tsp mustard seed · ½ tsp black peppercorns · 2 bay leaves · 2 garlic cloves · dill or chilli
  flakes to taste

Simmer 5 min, pour hot over 12 peeled hard-boiled eggs in a clean jar, lid on, fridge.

**Variations worth the jar:**
- **Pink beet eggs** — swap 1 cup of the water for beetroot juice and drop in a few vac-pack
  beets. Nitrates are already on your list for blood flow, and they look ridiculous in a good way.
- **Curry/turmeric eggs** — 1 tbsp curry powder + turmeric in the brine. Turmeric's on the
  debloat shelf anyway.
- **Pickle other things in the same jar** — red onion, carrot batons, cucumber, cauliflower. All
  near-zero calorie, all in [diet-foods.md], all fibre and crunch.

### The honest flag: don't do the soy/ramen-egg version right now

Ajitama (soy + mirin marinated jammy eggs) is the better-tasting jar and I'd normally push you at
it. Two problems for *you specifically*:
- It's **not preserved** — a soy marinade is low-acid, so it's a 3–4 day fridge item, not a
  big-jar-for-weeks item. It fails the thing you actually asked for.
- It's a **sodium bomb**, and water-retention bloat + facial puffiness is literally the thing
  you've named as the target. Soy-brining a dozen eggs is pulling directly against it.

Do it as an occasional 4-egg, 3-day batch when you fancy it. Don't make it the big jar.

### Macro impact
2 pickled eggs ≈ **144 kcal · P 13 · F 10 · C ~1** — the same as snack P3, because the brine is
near-zero. Slots into the plan with no recount needed.

### Add to the Lidl basket for this
- 2 × 1 L jars (or just save two passata jars — identical job, free)
- White vinegar ×2, cider vinegar ×1
- Mustard seed, bay leaves, dill (Kania range)
- Vac-pack beetroot
- Eggs ×24 (two jars' worth)
