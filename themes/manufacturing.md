## Log

### 2026-07-26
Japan's ¥80 trillion national robotics push (targeting 10 million AI robots by 2040, Toyota/Fujitsu/Kawasaki/FANUC now in Nvidia's Cosmos Coalition) is a bigger, more concrete industrial-policy commitment than anything comparable in the US. Combined with Boston Dynamics' simplified new Atlas (production-ready, ~30,000 units/yr target) and Figure's BMW pilot (Figure 03, ~1 robot/hour), July 2026 looks like the moment humanoid robotics started producing real if small unit volumes rather than demos. Musk's own admission that Optimus's ramp will be "long and flat" given a near-total lack of a mature component supply chain (~10,000 entirely new Gen3 parts) is a useful reality check — that supply chain (actuators, sensors, specialized components) is itself a research candidate as a picks-and-shovels angle, separate from the robot-makers themselves.
